# AWS Well-Architected Framework — Summary

The AWS Well-Architected Framework is a set of guidelines published by AWS to help teams evaluate and improve the design of their cloud workloads. It is organized around six pillars.

## Operational Excellence

Focuses on running and monitoring systems to deliver business value, and continually improving processes. This includes automating changes, responding to events, and defining standards for managing day-to-day operations.

## Security

Covers protecting information, systems, and assets through risk assessment and mitigation. Key practices include implementing a strong identity foundation, enabling traceability, applying security at every layer, and preparing for security events through incident response planning.

## Reliability

Addresses the ability of a workload to perform its intended function correctly and consistently, including the ability to recover quickly from failures. This includes designing for automatic recovery, testing recovery procedures, and scaling horizontally to increase overall availability.

## Performance Efficiency

Focuses on using computing resources efficiently to meet requirements, and maintaining that efficiency as demand changes and technologies evolve. This includes democratizing advanced technologies, going global in minutes, and experimenting more often through easily reversible infrastructure changes.

## Cost Optimization

Covers avoiding unnecessary costs, understanding spending over time, and selecting the most appropriate resource types and sizes. This includes adopting a consumption model, measuring overall efficiency, and analyzing and attributing expenditure.

## Sustainability

Addresses minimizing the environmental impact of running cloud workloads. This includes understanding the impact of a workload over its full lifecycle, setting sustainability goals, and maximizing utilization to minimize wasted resources.

## Using the Framework

Workloads are typically assessed pillar by pillar using a structured set of questions, producing a list of high-risk issues to address. Teams generally revisit the review periodically as a workload evolves, rather than treating it as a one-time exercise.
